Hi, it's Michał. I'm on a two-week camping trip with my wife and kids right now, tents, no fixed plans, the kind of holiday where the biggest decision some days is which lake to drive to. And somehow I still open Slack most mornings before I've properly woken up, and again some evenings after the kids are asleep.

I'm not on-call. Nobody is paging me. I checked anyway, mostly for three things: is the team okay, has anything changed in our infra that I'd want to know about before I'm back, and did any of my closed tickets get reopened because someone hit a constraint I didn't account for. That last one's the one that actually gets me, the idea that something I thought was done quietly isn't anymore, and I wouldn't know until I logged back in two weeks from now to find out.

One morning it paid off, sort of. Someone had left a mention on a ticket assigned to me, with a suggestion for how to solve it. I wasn't going to pick it up myself from a campsite, so I forwarded the advice to the engineer actually working on it and went back to making coffee over the stove. Small thing. Took two minutes. But it's exactly the kind of two minutes that, multiplied by fourteen days, adds up to never actually being away.

Nobody told me to do this, and nobody's tracking whether I answer DMs on holiday. It's more that two years of being the person who watches for things breaking has trained a reflex I didn't know I was building, and now it runs on its own even when there's genuinely nothing for me to catch.

That's the part that worries me more than the holiday itself. Burnout doesn't usually announce itself, from what I understand, it creeps in as this kind of checking becoming normal until you stop noticing you're doing it. I'm not on-call yet, and I'm already struggling to leave the phone in the tent for a full day. I don't know what that looks like once I actually am.

We're about halfway through the trip and honestly I think whatever prompted that ticket to reopen is probably solved by now. But I don't know, and for the first time this week I'm trying to leave it at that instead of checking.

Would be curious if you've found an actual way to switch this off, or if it's just something you carry once you've done this job long enough.
