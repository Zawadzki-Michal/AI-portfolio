Hi there. So I came across [this piece via Simon Willison's blog](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/hn-50421091/) — Paul Ford writing about how the web, as it exists, is more than enough. And I don't know, it caught me at the right moment I guess.

I've been spending a lot of time lately in Azure portals, Terraform configs, pipeline YAML, all the infrastructure stuff that sometimes feels very far from "the web" as a thing people actually use. You get deep enough into deployment automation and you can forget that what you're deploying is, eventually, just stuff that shows up in a browser for someone. A page. A form. An API response that something else turns into a page.

Ford's point — or what I took from it anyway — is that the basic architecture of the web, the way it actually works, is still good. Still enough. We keep layering complexity on top, chasing some shinier version of it, and maybe we don't need to. The web already does the thing.

I'm not sure I fully agree, or maybe I just don't know enough to have a strong opinion yet. I work with Azure because that's where the work is, and a lot of what I do involves making things more complicated so they can be more reliable, more scalable, more whatever. That's the job. But there's something in Ford's argument that sticks with me, this idea that the foundation isn't broken, we just keep forgetting it's there.

It's a weird thing to sit with when your day-to-day is all about adding layers. Infrastructure as code, CI/CD, monitoring, alerting, all the stuff that exists because "just put a file on a server" stopped being enough at some point. Or did it? I honestly don't know. Maybe the simple thing is still there underneath, and the complexity is real too, and they coexist without actually being in conflict. I've been thinking about this since Sunday evening, scrolling through the piece instead of going to bed at a reasonable hour.

The web is still the web. That's either obvious or profound depending on when you read it, I think.
