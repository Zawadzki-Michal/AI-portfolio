Hi there. So I spent part of my Sunday morning reading about [Markdown SVG upgrades](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/16/markdown-svg-upgrades/) on Simon Willison's blog, which is funny because I have absolutely zero practical use for this right now.

Let me be honest, I'm not building anything that needs fancy SVG rendering in Markdown. My day-to-day is Azure infrastructure, Terraform configs, maybe the occasional pipeline tweak. The closest I get to visual output is staring at monitoring dashboards or squinting at YAML indentation. And yet here I am, genuinely interested in how someone else is solving a problem I don't have.

I think that's part of what draws me to Simon's writing generally. He's always tinkering with something, documenting the messy middle of figuring it out, showing the work before it's clean. There's something about watching someone work through the details of a niche problem that makes me want to understand it even when it's miles from my own work. Maybe it's the process itself that's interesting, not the specific output.

The SVG stuff got me thinking about documentation more broadly though. At work I've been trying to get better at diagramming infrastructure, and I'm still pretty bad at it honestly. I can describe what's happening in words but turning that into something visual that actually helps someone else understand the system? That's a different skill entirely. I keep meaning to spend more time with Mermaid diagrams in our repos, because at least those live next to the code and don't rot as fast as a PNG someone made six months ago and never updated.

I don't know if there's a connection between what Simon's doing and what I need to learn. Probably I'm just looking for an excuse to justify the time I spent reading about it. But sometimes you read something that has nothing to do with your job and it shifts how you think about a problem you do have, even if you can't articulate exactly how.

Anyway, that's my Sunday morning. Now back to the actual work I should be doing.
