Hi there. Came across [this piece on Simon Willison's blog](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/23/anthropics-best-ai-model-struggles-to-attract-users-as-cheaper-t/) over the weekend and it's been rattling around in my head since.

The gist is that Anthropic's flagship model — apparently their most capable one — is struggling to find users because cheaper alternatives are good enough for most tasks. Which, okay, makes sense on paper. But it got me thinking about how I actually pick tools when I'm experimenting with AI stuff at work.

I'm not building anything sophisticated here. Mostly it's small automation tasks, occasionally asking a model to help me parse some Azure CLI output or draft a runbook. And honestly, I've never once thought "I need the absolute best model for this." I think about cost, I think about whether it'll be annoying to set up, and I think about whether it'll be roughly correct often enough that I'm not spending more time fixing its mistakes than I would have spent doing the thing manually.

So when I read about the "best" model struggling, my first reaction was kind of a shrug. But then I started wondering — am I missing something by defaulting to whatever's cheapest and easiest? Is there a class of problem I'm just not attempting because I've already decided it's not worth the token cost?

I genuinely don't know. My day-to-day is more infrastructure wrangling than pushing boundaries with language models. But the article made me a little uncomfortable, in a way I'm still sorting out. Like maybe I've gotten too comfortable treating AI tooling as interchangeable commodity plumbing when there's actually meaningful capability I'm leaving on the table.

Or maybe for what I'm doing, good enough really is good enough. The people who need the flagship model might be doing something fundamentally different than mucking about with infrastructure automation. I sat with that thought for a while on Sunday evening, half-watching something on TV, and couldn't quite settle it either way.

That nagging feeling is still there — I should probably test that assumption at some point instead of just reaching for cheaper by default. Maybe spin up something more ambitious on a weekend and see if the difference is actually noticeable.
