Hi again. I've been sitting with a question for a couple of weeks now and I don't think I've landed anywhere solid, so figured I'd just write through it instead of waiting until I did.

The question is whether AI is actually good for someone like me, a career changer still building the fundamentals, or whether it quietly takes away the exact struggle that used to build them. And right now I've got one example pulling each way.

The first one is at work. I switched off the GitHub Copilot web client a while back and moved into working in OpenCode in the terminal instead. With the web client, every session started from zero, I'd ask something, get an answer, close the tab, and next time I was back to re-explaining the same project and the same reasoning all over again. Once I wired up instructions, rules, and workflows in OpenCode, and ran a Graphify scan so it actually had the shape of the codebase instead of me describing it from memory, it stopped feeling like a tool I asked things and started feeling like a system that already knew what I was working on and why. That one's been a clear win. Less time re-explaining, more time actually deciding things.

The second one is messier, and it's the one I actually sat down to write about. I'm studying for my CKA right now, most evenings and a good chunk of my weekends, working through clusters, manifests, networking, RBAC, all of it by hand, the way the exam expects you to. No copy-pasting, no assistant, just me, a terminal, and whatever I've actually managed to retain.

And in the middle of that, I'll watch an AI agent investigate a broken cluster, work out what's wrong, write the fix, and wire the whole thing back together in about the time it takes me to read the error message properly. It's genuinely impressive. It's also demoralising in a way I wasn't expecting going in.

I'll be a few hours into practicing some networking scenario I've already gotten wrong twice, still not fully sure why a service isn't routing the way I expect, and there's a small voice asking what the actual point is if something else can do it faster and probably better before I've finished my coffee. That's not a comfortable thing to sit with when you've already paid for the exam and blocked out the study time around work and family.

Here's where I keep landing, though, even on the days it feels pointless. The exam itself won't let me ask an AI anything. And neither will a cluster that's down at whatever hour, with people waiting on me to actually understand what I'm looking at rather than waiting on a tool to explain it back to me. I know you can't blindly trust what AI hands you, and nothing I've seen so far replaces actually knowing the fundamentals well enough to catch it when it's wrong, or to know what question to even ask it in the first place. That's easy to say and harder to feel at ten at night when the CKA material still isn't sticking.

So I'm still studying. I just haven't fully made peace with doing it in a world where the thing I'm grinding to learn, something else can already do without blinking. Maybe that feeling goes away once I'm the one who can tell when it's wrong. Maybe it doesn't.

There's a related thing nagging at me too, about whether all this is quietly changing how teams actually share knowledge, whether pairing with someone next to you starts losing out to just asking a bot instead. That one's a whole separate post though, not something to squeeze onto the end of this one.
